Research with students

Could a GAN generate useful training data? Could a classifier choose what to learn next? Research I conceived, developed, supervised or reviewed with Machine Learning Practical (MLP) and MSc students during my PhD and postdoc years.

I proposed many of these directions and helped with architectures, experiments and code. Some grew into further research; others were useful explorations or projects I reviewed. Each entry separates the idea, my contribution and what the work actually established.

Detailed case study

Let the model choose its augmentations

Why keep sampling augmentations blindly when the model can tell us which ones it currently finds difficult?

  • EvolutionAugment
  • augmentation
  • curriculum
  • image classification
The project, my role & discussion points

What we explored

EvolutionAugment samples candidate augmentation policies, evaluates them against the current classifier, and selects difficult examples for the training update. The selection changes as the model learns. We explored selection for individual examples as well as whole batches.

My part

I mentored the project through regular meetings and technical guidance. The work developed from one of the MSc directions I was supervising; the student built and evaluated the method.

Where it got to

An implemented method, classification experiments and an MSc dissertation. Evaluating more candidates adds forward-pass cost, so augmentation quality has to be considered alongside training throughput.

Worth discussing

  • The difference between an example being difficult and being useful to learn from.
  • When per-example policy selection earns its additional computation.

Detailed case study

Image translation without starting from scratch

Can we reuse what large pretrained models already know to learn a new visual translation from much less data?

  • CLIP
  • StyleGAN2
  • CycleGAN
  • low-data learning
The project, my role & discussion points

What we explored

The project combined CLIP representations, a pretrained StyleGAN2 generator and a learned translation module. A CycleGAN-style objective encouraged consistency in CLIP feature space. Earlier experiments with DALL-E components helped narrow the architecture to Style-CycleGAN.

My part

I provided weekly supervision and technical suggestions as the architecture and experiments developed. The student implemented and evaluated the translation systems; we were reusing pretrained components, not training those foundation models from scratch.

Where it got to

Implemented architectures and an MSc dissertation. Some routes did not work, and numerical image-quality measures did not settle every question about whether the translated image preserved the right content.

Worth discussing

  • What a feature-space cycle loss preserves, and what it can let disappear.
  • Why freezing weights does not necessarily remove the cost of backpropagating through a model.

Detailed case study

Adversarially choosing the training view

Instead of attacking individual pixels, what if the adversary searched over meaningful transformations of an image?

  • adversarial learning
  • differentiable augmentation
  • robustness
The project, my role & discussion points

What we explored

The project made augmentations such as rotation, masked noise and cutout differentiable, then optimised their parameters to challenge the classifier. Constraints on those transformations mattered: an image that no longer contains the class is a rather different training problem.

My part

I proposed the direction and provided the main day-to-day supervision. I also supplied an existing augmentation-code resource and helped the student reason about the implementation and experiments.

Where it got to

Implemented methods and image-classification experiments documented in a dissertation. The inner optimisation made training cost an important part of the comparison.

Worth discussing

  • How to make an augmentation difficult without destroying the information needed to label it.
  • Comparing robustness at equal wall-clock cost, not just equal numbers of epochs.

Detailed case study

Which examples should an imbalanced classifier see?

Can the model's current mistakes guide a curriculum towards classes it is neglecting?

  • class imbalance
  • curriculum
  • hard-example mining
  • ViT
The project, my role & discussion points

What we explored

We investigated loss-driven curricula for imbalanced image classification. The implemented procedure cached losses, ranked examples and selected a subset for backward passes, with comparisons across convolutional networks and a vision transformer.

My part

I supervised the dissertation and helped with technical direction and experimental design. That included thinking through baselines, data splits and the difference between selecting difficult examples and balancing classes.

Where it got to

A completed dissertation with mixed findings. Training loss alone was not a dependable way to repair class imbalance; its usefulness depended on the setting.

Worth discussing

  • High loss can mean a useful learning opportunity, insufficient exposure, or an unhelpful outlier. How do we distinguish them?
  • Controlling the backward-pass budget when comparing a curriculum with class-balanced sampling.

Detailed case study

DamageGAN: generate the damage we rarely see

Could synthetic satellite images help a classifier recognise underrepresented levels of building damage?

  • conditional GANs
  • satellite imagery
  • class imbalance
  • evaluation
The project, my role & discussion points

What we explored

A class-conditioned pix2pix model generated building-damage imagery from the xBD dataset. The useful test was whether adding those images helped a downstream damage classifier, rather than whether the samples simply looked convincing.

My part

I supervised the team and helped shape the evaluation: classifier comparisons, augmentation controls, repeated measurements and separating transfer-learning effects from the contribution of generated data.

Where it got to

An implemented project and manuscript. The study did not establish a clear overall classification improvement. That left an important question: was missing data really the bottleneck, or was the classifier failing to use the surrounding scene?

Worth discussing

  • Why visually realistic samples can still be poor training data.
  • The circular difficulty of learning to generate a class for which we have very few real examples.

About the catalogue

MLP is Edinburgh’s Machine Learning Practical course. This catalogue brings together recovered projects, research discussions and review contributions, not only publications. Detailed case studies have fuller accounts of the work; research notes preserve narrower evidence. Review contributions are identified separately. The entry count is not a count of projects I personally implemented or teams I formally supervised.

Discussion points are prompts for exploring the work, not claims that every suggested experiment was carried out. Records without an established topic remain in the private archive while I reconstruct them; this is not an exhaustive census of every project.

The 2021 MLP projects sit in my PhD-to-postdoc transition year and are grouped with the earlier MLP work. Student reports, private correspondence and assessment records stay private. Public papers and code are linked where available.
